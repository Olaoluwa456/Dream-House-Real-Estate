A listing is the beginning of your due diligence, not a replacement for it.

Ask a question ↗

Contact the agency with the property reference, preferred date and the people attending. Confirm the address and appointment directly before travelling. For this sample site, listings are fictional examples and no real inspection can be booked.

Documents vary by property and transaction. Ask an independent Nigerian property lawyer to review the title chain, registered interests, survey, planning status, approvals, seller authority and relevant search results. Do not rely on screenshots or a listing description as proof of ownership.

Depending on the deal, there may be agency or professional fees, legal fees, registration costs, taxes or levies, rent, caution deposit, service charge and utility costs. Ask for an itemised written breakdown and confirm the contractual basis and applicable law before paying.

Confirm the landlord or authorised agent's identity and authority, view the property, read the proposed tenancy agreement, understand payment terms and all additional charges, and get a receipt for every payment. Avoid sending money solely because of an online listing or urgent messaging.

Remote enquiries can begin with clear written requirements and a video call, but arrange independent local inspections and legal review. Confirm the identity and authority of every person involved, and use properly documented payment channels.
